## **GRIDRUNNER: Pulse**

**Core hook:** You're a light-cycle-style ship racing along procedurally generated neon tracks that are *generated from music* (like Audiosurf), but instead of just dodging/collecting blocks, you're fighting off enemy ships that spawn in rhythm with the beat — and every run is a fresh roguelike build.

### How the genres combine

**Tron (movement & aesthetic)**
- You ride a light-trail vehicle on a glowing grid. Your trail is solid — cross it and you die, same for enemies crossing theirs.
- The track itself is the level: no free-roam, you're always moving forward at a speed tied to the song's BPM.

**Audiosurf (procedural generation from music)**
- Drop in a song (or pick from a curated set), and the track's turns, jumps, and hazard density are generated from the waveform — bass drops become ramps/loops, quiet sections become narrow tightropes, drum hits spawn enemy waves.
- Speed scales with tempo, so a 170 BPM track is a completely different experience from a slow 90 BPM one.

**Shooter**
- You've got a forward-firing weapon and limited turning, so aiming is really about *positioning* on the grid — cutting an enemy off with your trail while shooting is often better than just shooting.
- Enemies fire back in patterns synced to the music, so dodging bullets *is* the rhythm game.

**Roguelike**
- Between "checkpoint" segments (song sections), you choose from 3 random upgrades — trail effects (explosive trail, trail that slows enemies), weapon mods (spread shot, homing, piercing), or ship mods (wall-run, dash, temporary invincibility on beat-perfect turns).
- Runs are permadeath: die, and you restart the song from the beginning with a fresh seed and no upgrades.
- Meta-progression unlocks new starting weapons, cosmetic trail colors, and remixes/harder difficulty versions of tracks.

### The loop
1. Pick a song → track generates.
2. Race/fight through it, banking "sync meter" for playing well on-beat (perfect turns, beat-timed kills).
3. Sync meter fills → triggers a roguelike upgrade choice at each section break.
4. Reach the end of the song = boss section (a big enemy ship whose attack pattern is the song's chorus/hook).
5. Score attack + unlocks feed back into a persistent meta-layer.

### Why it'd feel fresh
Most rhythm/procedural-music games (Audiosurf, Beat Saber) are about *reacting*, not *building a character*. Layering roguelike upgrade choices on top means skilled players get snowball runs where late-song sections feel like power fantasies, while the Tron trail mechanic gives you an actual *offensive* tool besides shooting — you can out-maneuver enemies into their own deaths.
